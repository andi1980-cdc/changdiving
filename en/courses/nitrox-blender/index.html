Nitrox Gas Blender Course in Koh Chang – Professional Gas Mixing Training

The Nitrox Gas Blender Course at Koh Chang taught by certified TDI and PADI Instructors takes you deep into the technical foundation of recreational and technical diving: safe, precise and professional nitrox gas blending.

This course is designed for divers, dive professionals and dive center staff who want to fully understand how nitrox is produced, analyzed and safely filled. You will learn exactly how an empty cylinder becomes a precisely blended nitrox mix – in compliance with international standards.

Who Should Take the Nitrox Gas Blender Course?

This course is ideal for:

  • Dive center staff involved in filling and gas handling
  • Instructors and Divemasters looking to expand their technical skills
  • Certified nitrox divers interested in the science and practice of gas blending
  • Divers planning to move toward advanced nitrox and technical diving

No marketing fluff – this is real-world, hands-on training using professional equipment in an operational dive center environment at Koh Chang.

Course Prerequisites

  • Minimum age: 18 years
  • Strong interest in diving operations, safety and gas management

What You Will Learn

The Nitrox Gas Blender Course provides in-depth theoretical and practical knowledge, including:

  • Responsibilities and legal duties of a Nitrox Gas Blender
  • Gases used in scuba diving: air, oxygen and nitrogen
  • Oxygen handling procedures and oxygen fire prevention
  • Oxygen system design and local regulations
  • Gas production equipment: compressors, cylinders, filtration and gauges
  • Mixing techniques:
    • Partial pressure blending
    • Continuous blending systems
    • Pre-mix and de-nitrogenated air systems
  • Oxygen analysis procedures and correct use of analyzers
  • Cylinder labeling, documentation and fill logs

A strong focus is placed on understanding gas mathematics and safe operating limits, directly connected to real dive planning and gas consumption considerations.

Practical Training & Certification Requirements

During the course you will:

  • Blend and analyze a minimum of 5 nitrox cylinders
  • Achieve oxygen accuracy within ±1% of the target mix
  • Complete proper nitrox fill logs including MOD and oxygen percentage
  • Pass the Nitrox Gas Blender written examination

Upon successful completion, graduates are qualified to blend nitrox gases independently, without direct instructor supervision.

Interactive blending calculators

These tools follow standard textbook assumptions: ideal gas, topping gas is air with 21% O₂, and all cylinder pressures are absolute bar (atmospheric + gauge if you work in gauge units—stay consistent with your training). Continuous-flow mode assumes the incoming mix blends uniformly with the gas already in the cylinder while you fill.

⚠️ Important: For education and classroom practice only. No warranty. Real fills require a certified blender, oxygen-clean equipment, an oxygen-compatible compressor and breathing-air supply suited to nitrox/O₂ use, correct procedures, and analyzer verification. Follow your agency manual—fill order and local rules may differ.

Hazards: Nitrox and oxygen blending involve fire and explosion risk. Getting it wrong can threaten lives and property. Blend nitrox only if you are certified as a gas blender (this course qualifies you to blend independently afterward). Always use all safety standards of the scuba diving industry and respect local laws—on every fill.

Partial pressure blending (O₂ first, then air)

Note: All equipment used must be oxygen-clean. The topping air must be oxygen-compatible.

Empty: pressure to reach with pure O₂ before topping with air to final pressure. Residual: pressure after the O₂ step before air top-off.

Enter values to calculate the O₂ step pressure.

Continuous flow blending (inlet mix)

Required O₂ fraction in the continuous fill gas so that, after filling from the start state to final pressure, the cylinder matches the target mix (simple mixing model).

Real-world note: Continuous-flow panels often need a slightly higher O₂ setpoint than this ideal figure, because of plumbing, mixing path, fill rate, and where you sample. At our station, for example, landing on EAN32 typically means dialling in around 33.5% O₂ on the blender—not because the math is wrong, but because the installation behaves that way. Always analyse every cylinder and adjust your workflow to your system.

Enter values for required inlet O₂%.

MOD (maximum operating depth)

Depth at which ambient pressure reaches your chosen maximum inspired O₂ partial pressure (freshwater / common metric approximation: 1 bar per 10 m).

Enter O₂% and max PPO₂ for MOD.
